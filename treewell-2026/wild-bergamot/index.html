Wild Bergamot

  • TREEWELL-2026
  • WILD-BERGAMOT

Monarda fistulosa

Seed Mixes: Northeast Native

About

Wild Bergamot is one of the most widespread and adaptable native wildflowers in North America, found in dry to medium open areas, prairies, and roadsides from Canada to Mexico and across most of the continent. It grows 2–4 feet tall with shaggy, lavender-pink flower heads surrounded by pale, pinkish-tinged bracts (the small leaves just below the flower cluster). The entire plant is intensely aromatic — the foliage smells remarkably like oregano, to which it is related, and was used historically to make a herbal tea. It is related to garden bee balm (Monarda didyma) but more drought-tolerant and more adaptable to dry conditions.

Wildlife Value

Wild Bergamot is one of the premier pollinator plants for midsummer. Bumblebees work the flowers continuously, hummingbird moths hover at them in the evening, tiger swallowtails and fritillaries visit, and ruby-throated hummingbirds are frequent callers. The aromatic leaves may deter some herbivorous insects. Historically the leaves were brewed as tea, used as flavoring, and employed medicinally by many Native American communities.

In the Treewell

Wild Bergamot spreads by rhizome to form a colony over time, which will help it fill space in the treewell and crowd out weeds. It is significantly more drought-tolerant than red bee balm (Monarda didyma) and will handle the dry conditions of a treewell more reliably. Good air circulation — which a treewell typically provides — helps reduce any tendency toward powdery mildew in humid conditions. The fragrant lavender blooms in July and August are a midsummer highlight.

Growing Notes

  • Type: Perennial
  • Height: 2–4 ft
  • Bloom time: July–September
  • Sun needs: Full sun to part shade
  • Water needs: Low to moderate; drought tolerant once established

Seedling Identification

Cotyledons are small and oval. The first true leaves are oval to lance-shaped, opposite, and noticeably hairy and rough — the texture is more coarse than Lemon Mint or Lavender Hyssop. The square stem of the mint family is present early. The definitive feature is scent: crushing a leaf releases a strong oregano-like herbal fragrance (Wild Bergamot is closely related to culinary oregano). If the seedling smells strongly of oregano and has a square stem, it’s almost certainly Wild Bergamot.
