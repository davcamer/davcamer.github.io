## About

Black-Eyed Susan is one of the most recognizable wildflowers in eastern and central North America, native to prairies, open woods, and roadsides from Canada to Florida. It produces cheerful golden-yellow daisy-like flowers with a distinctive dark brown central cone, typically standing 1–3 feet tall. Though technically a short-lived perennial, it behaves like an annual or biennial in many gardens — but self-seeds so freely that it reliably returns year after year. It is one of the most adaptable and forgiving wildflowers you can plant.

## Wildlife Value

Black-Eyed Susan is a major nectar source for native bees, butterflies, and beetles throughout its long bloom season. The seeds are eagerly eaten by goldfinches, chickadees, and other songbirds — leave the seedheads standing through fall and winter for maximum benefit. It also serves as a host plant for several moth species, including the Silvery Checkerspot butterfly caterpillar.

## In the Treewell

Black-Eyed Susan should be one of the first things to bloom in the treewell, potentially flowering in its first summer if seed was sown early enough. It tolerates urban heat, compacted soil, and drought better than most wildflowers, making it an excellent performer in a treewell context. Expect it to self-seed into surrounding cracks and gaps, naturalizing the space over time.

## Growing Notes

- **Type:** Short-lived perennial (often grown as annual or biennial)
- **Height:** 1–3 ft
- **Bloom time:** June–October
- **Sun needs:** Full sun to part shade
- **Water needs:** Low to moderate; drought tolerant once established

## Seedling Identification

Cotyledons are small and oval. The first true leaves are oval to broadly lance-shaped with slightly toothed margins and a distinctly rough, sandpapery texture — covered in short stiff hairs that feel scratchy to the touch. The leaves are opposite at first, turning alternate as the plant grows. That scratchy hairiness is the key feature; common weeds like lamb's quarters and chickweed have smooth, soft leaves by comparison.
