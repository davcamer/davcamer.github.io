*Penstemon hirsutus*

**Seed Mixes:** Eastern Pollinator

---

## About

Hairy Beardtongue is a native perennial of the northeastern United States, found in rocky, dry, open woods and clearings. It is one of the few penstemons native to the Northeast and one of the most adaptable to the region's conditions. Growing 1–3 feet tall, it produces tall racemes of pale lavender to white tubular flowers with soft purple veining, which bloom in late spring. The plant is covered in fine hairs — the source of both its common and scientific names (*hirsutus* means "hairy") — which help it tolerate drought by reducing water loss.

## Wildlife Value

Like other penstemons, Hairy Beardtongue attracts bumblebees, mason bees, and specialist native bees in the genus *Osmia*. Ruby-throated hummingbirds visit the tubular flowers. The persistent evergreen basal rosette provides visual interest and low-level ground cover through winter, and the dried seed capsules provide food for birds during the colder months.

## In the Treewell

Hairy Beardtongue is genuinely well-suited to an urban treewell. Its tolerance of dry, poor, rocky soils mirrors the conditions that stress many garden plants, and its hairy stems and leaves give it a natural drought resistance. The persistent basal rosette means it offers some winter greenery in the treewell, and the spring flower stalks are elegant without being showy — a subtle but valuable plant.

## Growing Notes

- **Type:** Perennial
- **Height:** 1–3 ft
- **Bloom time:** May–July
- **Sun needs:** Full sun to part shade
- **Water needs:** Low; tolerates dry, rocky, poor soils

## Seedling Identification

Cotyledons are oval. The first true leaves are oval to lance-shaped, opposite, and distinctly hairy — the hairs are visible to the naked eye and give the leaves a soft, slightly fuzzy texture. This hairiness immediately distinguishes Hairy Beardtongue from Foxglove Beardtongue, which has smooth leaves. The seedlings form a low rosette of opposite hairy leaves. Leaves are a medium green.
