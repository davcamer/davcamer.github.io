*Baptisia australis*

**Seed Mixes:** Eastern Pollinator, Northeast Native

## About

Wild Blue Indigo is widely considered one of the most beautiful and valuable native perennials available to northeastern gardeners. Native to open woods and prairies of eastern and central North America, it eventually forms a large, bushy, shrub-like clump 3–5 feet tall and wide with attractive blue-green foliage. In May and June it produces tall, showy racemes of indigo-blue pea-like flowers that are stunning in the garden and excellent for cut flowers. After the flowers fade, the inflated, charcoal-black seedpods rattle audibly when shaken in the wind — earning the plant the old common name "rattleweed." It is among the most long-lived of all native perennials.

## Wildlife Value

Wild Blue Indigo is a host plant for the Wild Indigo Duskywing skipper, the Frosted Elfin butterfly, and the Orange Sulphur butterfly — all of which depend on *Baptisia* species for their larval stage. It is an important early-season nectar source for bumblebees. The rattling seedpods are not just charming — the seeds inside are eaten by some wildlife. A plant that supports specialists is always particularly valuable ecologically.

## In the Treewell

Wild Blue Indigo requires patience above all else — it is slow to establish, potentially taking 3 or more years to reach its full, impressive size, and it resents being moved or disturbed once planted. The deep taproot it develops in those first years is what makes it ultimately so drought tolerant and long-lived. Think of it as a long-term investment: a plant that, once established, will be a fixture in the treewell for decades with virtually no maintenance.

## Growing Notes

- **Type:** Perennial
- **Height:** 3–5 ft
- **Bloom time:** May–June
- **Sun needs:** Full sun to light shade
- **Water needs:** Low once established; deep taproot provides drought tolerance

## Seedling Identification

Cotyledons are thick, oval, and noticeably fleshy. The first true leaves are trifoliate — three smooth, oval to oblong leaflets arranged like a clover but with longer, more elongated leaflets. The leaves are alternate and a distinctive blue-green to grayish-green color. Unlike clovers, Wild Blue Indigo leaflets are smooth, clearly not round, and the overall seedling has an upright, somewhat stiff bearing. The blue-green color and smooth texture of the trifoliate leaves are the key features.
