*Dalea purpurea*

**Seed Mixes:** Bird Lover's

## About

Purple Prairie Clover is a native perennial legume of the central North American prairies, growing 1–3 feet tall with fine, airy foliage composed of tiny leaflets. The flowers are distinctive: small, bright purple blooms arranged on a cylindrical spike, blooming in a ring that begins at the base of the spike and slowly moves upward as the season progresses — an unusual and visually interesting pattern. As a legume, it fixes atmospheric nitrogen in the soil, improving soil fertility. It is extremely drought tolerant once established.

## Wildlife Value

Purple Prairie Clover is highly attractive to bumblebees, digger bees, and butterflies. It is the host plant for specialist bees in the genera *Dalea*-specialist *Andrena* and *Pseudopanurgus*, bees that depend specifically on this plant's pollen. Seeds are eaten by birds and small mammals. The deep root system and nitrogen-fixing capability improve the soil ecosystem for surrounding plants.

## In the Treewell

Prairie Clover may be slow to establish in its first year, but it is long-lived and drought tolerant once it settles in. The unusual flowering pattern — a ring of bloom moving slowly up the spike — is something visitors are likely to notice and ask about. The fine-textured, airy foliage adds a delicate quality to the planting that contrasts with bolder-leaved species. Its prairie origins make it well-adapted to the lean, hot conditions of an urban treewell.

## Growing Notes

- **Type:** Perennial
- **Height:** 1–3 ft
- **Bloom time:** July–August
- **Sun needs:** Full sun
- **Water needs:** Low; highly drought tolerant once established

## Seedling Identification

Cotyledons are small and oval. The first true leaves are pinnately compound with several pairs of small, oval to oblong leaflets — similar in structure to a tiny vetch or clover, but with more leaflet pairs and an alternate arrangement. Hold a leaf up to the light and you can see tiny translucent oil glands dotting the leaflets — these glandular dots are a signature Dalea feature. Crushing a leaf releases a faint spicy or aromatic scent from those glands. The alternate compound leaf with glandular dots distinguishes it from clovers, which have trifoliate leaves in an alternate arrangement.
