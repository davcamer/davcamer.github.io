## About

Four-O'Clocks are a tender perennial from South America, grown as an annual in the Northeast, with a long history of cultivation in gardens around the world. They produce bushy plants 18–36 inches tall covered in fragrant, trumpet-shaped flowers in vivid shades of magenta, yellow, white, and striped combinations — sometimes multiple colors on the same plant. True to their name, the flowers open in late afternoon (around 4 pm) and stay open through the night, closing again by late morning. Long cultivated, they have naturalized in many temperate regions.

## Wildlife Value

The evening opening of the flowers is a strong signal that sphinx moths (hawkmoths) are the primary pollinators — their long tongues can reach the nectar at the base of the tube, and they're active at the same hours the flowers are open. Hummingbirds also visit the flowers. Note that the seeds and roots of Four-O'Clocks can be toxic to humans and pets.

## In the Treewell

Four-O'Clocks are reliable warm-season performers that bloom from midsummer until frost without much fuss. The evening flowers and fragrance make them particularly appealing from a human-experience perspective — a nice feature in a building-front treewell. They grow well in urban conditions and may develop tuber-like storage roots in the soil that help them endure dry spells.

## Growing Notes

- **Type:** Tender perennial (grown as annual in Northeast)
- **Height:** 18–36 in
- **Bloom time:** July–October
- **Sun needs:** Full sun to part shade
- **Water needs:** Moderate; tolerates some drought

## Seedling Identification

Cotyledons are notably large, broad, and fleshy — one of the bigger seedling cotyledons you'll encounter. The first true leaves are broadly oval to slightly heart-shaped, opposite, smooth, and deep green with a somewhat glossy surface. The combination of large fleshy cotyledons followed by opposite, smooth, heart-shaped leaves is quite distinctive. Growth is vigorous once started.
