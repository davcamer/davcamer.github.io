*Monarda punctata*

**Seed Mixes:** Eastern Pollinator

## About

Spotted Bee Balm — also called Dotted Horsemint — is one of the most architecturally unusual wildflowers in the eastern and central United States. Native to dry sandy soils and open areas, it grows 1–3 feet tall and produces stacked whorls of tubular flowers in yellow with purple spots, surrounded by showy pink-purple to white bracts (modified leaves) that persist long after the flowers themselves have faded, extending the visual display well into late summer. The entire plant is intensely aromatic — the foliage smells strongly of thyme and oregano, which are relatives in the mint family. It functions as an annual or short-lived perennial depending on conditions.

## Wildlife Value

Spotted Bee Balm is a specialist bumblebee plant par excellence — bumblebees work the whorled flowers intensively. It also attracts a wide diversity of other native bees, butterflies, and wasps. The showy bracts attract pollinators even when the actual flowers are not in bloom. It supports specialist native bees that depend specifically on *Monarda* for pollen.

## In the Treewell

Spotted Bee Balm is one of the most visually striking plants in the mix, and one of the most unusual-looking wildflowers available. The stacked architecture of flower whorls and bracts creates a look that stops people in their tracks. It prefers the dry, lean, or sandy soils found in treewells, potentially doing better here than in a rich garden bed. It may self-seed to persist from year to year.

## Growing Notes

- **Type:** Annual or short-lived perennial
- **Height:** 1–3 ft
- **Bloom time:** July–September
- **Sun needs:** Full sun
- **Water needs:** Low; prefers dry, well-drained, lean soils

## Seedling Identification

Cotyledons are small and oval. The first true leaves are lance-shaped to oval, opposite, and covered in fine hairs — a typical mint-family seedling. The square stem of the mint family develops within the first few leaf pairs; run a finger along the stem and you can feel the four flat sides. Crush a leaf to release a strong aromatic scent — similar to oregano with a slightly resinous quality. The square stem and strong aromatic scent together confirm a Monarda species; Spotted Bee Balm's foliage tends to be more hairy than Lemon Mint.
