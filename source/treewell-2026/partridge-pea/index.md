*Chamaecrista fasciculata*

**Seed Mixes:** Bird Lover's, Eastern Pollinator, Northeast Native

## About

Partridge Pea is a native annual legume of eastern and central North America, found in open sandy or disturbed habitats, roadsides, and meadow edges. It grows 1–3 feet tall with attractive, finely divided feathery compound leaves and bright yellow five-petaled flowers with prominent dark anthers. One delightful characteristic: the leaves fold up when touched, like those of the sensitive plant (*Mimosa*). As a legume, it fixes atmospheric nitrogen into the soil, improving fertility for surrounding plants. It blooms prolifically from July through September and produces long seed pods.

## Wildlife Value

Partridge Pea is a host plant for several sulfur butterfly species — Cloudless Sulphur, Orange Sulphur, and Little Yellow butterflies all use it as a larval food plant. The leaves have extra-floral nectaries (small nectar-producing glands on the leaf stalks) that attract ants, which in turn protect the plant from herbivores. The seeds are eaten by bobwhite quail, wild turkey, and many songbirds — the common name comes from the bobwhite's fondness for the seeds.

## In the Treewell

Partridge Pea is a fast-growing annual that establishes easily from seed and blooms reliably in its first summer. It improves the soil through nitrogen fixation, which benefits surrounding perennials. The sensitive, touch-folding leaves are an interactive feature that makes people stop and investigate. It self-seeds to persist in the treewell in subsequent years.

## Growing Notes

- **Type:** Annual
- **Height:** 1–3 ft
- **Bloom time:** July–September
- **Sun needs:** Full sun
- **Water needs:** Low; tolerates dry, sandy soils

## Seedling Identification

Partridge Pea has one of the most distinctive seedlings in the mix, thanks to a behavior that's already present at the seedling stage. The cotyledons are oval and fleshy. The first true leaves are pinnately compound — divided into many small, oblong leaflets arranged in pairs along a central stalk, like a tiny fern frond. Touch the leaflets gently and they fold together (the same sensitive response as a Mimosa or "sensitive plant"). This folding-leaf behavior is unmistakable and makes Partridge Pea impossible to confuse with any weed.
