## About

Shasta Daisy is a hybrid perennial developed by American plant breeder Luther Burbank in the late 1800s, crossing several European daisy species to produce the classic large, white-petaled daisy with a golden-yellow center that has become a garden staple worldwide. Plants grow 2–3 feet tall and produce the archetypal daisy flower on long stems — the kind of flower children draw when they think of a daisy. It is not a native North American wildflower but has been grown in gardens for over a century. Named for Mount Shasta in California, where Burbank did much of his breeding work.

## Wildlife Value

Shasta Daisy has moderate wildlife value compared to the native wildflowers in the mix. Bees and beetles visit the flowers for pollen and nectar, and some birds eat the seeds. However, it is less valuable than native daisy relatives for specialist insects that have co-evolved with North American plants. It earns its place in the mix primarily for its visual contribution and its ability to attract generalist pollinators.

## In the Treewell

Shasta Daisy is very easy to grow and tolerates a range of conditions, making it a reliable contributor of classic white daisy flowers from early to midsummer. The white flowers provide a pleasing contrast to the yellows, oranges, and purples of the native wildflowers around them. It may spread by rhizome and self-sow, potentially needing some thinning every few years to keep it in check.

## Growing Notes

- **Type:** Perennial
- **Height:** 2–3 ft
- **Bloom time:** June–August
- **Sun needs:** Full sun
- **Water needs:** Moderate; average soils

## Seedling Identification

Cotyledons are small and oval. The first true leaves are broadly spatula- to spoon-shaped with rounded, shallow teeth along the margins, smooth, and a deep glossy green. The spoon-shaped leaves with rounded teeth and smooth, somewhat shiny surface are distinctive compared to the rough-textured native daisy-family seedlings in the mix. Leaves are alternate and form a low rosette. The smooth glossy texture is the most reliable distinguishing feature.
