*Symphyotrichum laeve*

**Seed Mixes:** Northeast Native

## About

Smooth Aster is one of the most elegant native asters of central and eastern North America, found in dry to medium prairies and open woods. It grows 1–4 feet tall — more compact than the towering New England Aster — with distinctive smooth, blue-green foliage that has a slightly waxy or powdery texture, completely free of the hairiness that characterizes most other asters. From late summer through fall it produces masses of blue-violet daisy-like flowers with golden-yellow centers. The smooth blue-green leaves are ornamentally attractive throughout the entire growing season, not just at bloom time.

## Wildlife Value

Smooth Aster is a critical late-season pollinator resource, providing nectar alongside New England Aster for the same community of bees, butterflies, and other insects that depend on fall-blooming plants. It supports monarch butterfly migration and is a host plant for the Pearl Crescent butterfly. The seeds are eaten by birds through fall and winter. Its earlier bloom time (relative to some asters) and smaller stature make it a useful complement to its larger relative in the treewell.

## In the Treewell

Smooth Aster is more compact and drought-tolerant than New England Aster, making it somewhat better suited to a lean, dry treewell. The smooth blue-green foliage is one of the most attractive in the mix — distinctive and ornamental from spring through fall. It pairs beautifully with goldenrods for the classic fall native combination of gold and blue-violet that defines the northeastern autumn landscape.

## Growing Notes

- **Type:** Perennial
- **Height:** 1–4 ft
- **Bloom time:** August–October
- **Sun needs:** Full sun
- **Water needs:** Low to moderate; drought tolerant once established

## Seedling Identification

Cotyledons are small and oval. The first true leaves are lance- to oval-shaped, alternate, and — as the name promises — distinctly smooth and hairless. The leaves also have a blue-green cast that is noticeable compared to most other seedlings. This combination of smooth hairless texture and blue-green color is the key ID feature, and it distinguishes Smooth Aster immediately from New England Aster, which has rough hairy leaves. The smooth blue-green leaves are one of the more elegant seedlings in the mix.
