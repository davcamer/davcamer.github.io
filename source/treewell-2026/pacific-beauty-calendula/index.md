*Calendula officinalis* 'Pacific Beauty'

**Seed Mixes:** Bird Lover's

## About

Calendula — also called Pot Marigold — is one of the oldest cultivated herbs, native to the Mediterranean region and long used in cooking, medicine, and cosmetics. The 'Pacific Beauty' strain is a particularly large-flowered selection, producing showy 3–4 inch blooms in shades of orange, yellow, and cream on plants 18–24 inches tall. The flowers are edible — petals can be used as a saffron substitute or added to salads. Calendula is a cool-season annual, performing best in spring and fall rather than the heat of midsummer.

## Wildlife Value

Calendula is a rich pollen source for bees, including honeybees, bumblebees, and native bees. It supports hoverflies (syrphid flies), which are important predators of aphids in their larval stage and significant pollinators as adults. The sticky stems of calendula are sometimes noted as incidentally trapping small soft-bodied insects, though the plant is not carnivorous.

## In the Treewell

Calendula will shine in the treewell during spring and fall when temperatures are cooler. It may slow significantly or stop flowering in the heat of July and August, but often rebounds with fresh flowers as temperatures drop in September. Deadheading spent flowers regularly will extend the bloom period significantly. The large, bright flowers are cheerful and colorful from the street.

## Growing Notes

- **Type:** Annual
- **Height:** 18–24 in
- **Bloom time:** May–October; best in cool weather (spring and fall)
- **Sun needs:** Full sun
- **Water needs:** Moderate; regular water extends bloom

## Seedling Identification

Cotyledons are large, narrow, and strap-shaped — quite elongated compared to most seedlings. The first true leaves are spatula- to paddle-shaped, alternate, and covered in fine hairs that give them a slightly sticky feel — touch a leaf and it may lightly adhere to your fingertip. The leaves also have a faint resinous or herbal scent. The sticky hairy surface and paddle-shaped alternate leaves are the key features. The plant forms a rosette before bolting.
