## About

Golden Alexander is a spring-blooming perennial native to moist meadows, open woods, and stream banks throughout eastern North America. It is a member of the carrot family (Apiaceae), producing bright yellow flat-topped flower clusters (umbels) on plants 2–3 feet tall. It is one of the earliest native wildflowers to bloom in the Northeast — a welcome burst of color in April and May before most other wildflowers are up. The attractive compound foliage remains fresh and ornamental throughout the season.

## Wildlife Value

Golden Alexander is most valuable as an early-season nectar source, providing food for native bees that emerge in spring before most other flowers are available. It is a host plant for the Black Swallowtail butterfly — caterpillars feed on the foliage as they do on related plants like parsley and dill. Specialist bees in the *Andrena* genus are particularly associated with carrot-family plants including Golden Alexander.

## In the Treewell

Golden Alexander's early bloom time fills an important niche in the treewell's seasonal sequence, providing interest from April onward. It tolerates partial shade, making it a good performer under street trees or in spots that receive morning sun and afternoon shade. In a very dry treewell, some supplemental watering in its first season will help it establish.

## Growing Notes

- **Type:** Perennial
- **Height:** 2–3 ft
- **Bloom time:** April–June
- **Sun needs:** Full sun to part shade
- **Water needs:** Moderate; prefers average to slightly moist soils

## Seedling Identification

Cotyledons are narrow to oval. The first true leaves are compound, divided into several toothed leaflets arranged pinnately, with a bright green color and a slightly glossy surface. The compound, toothed leaflets give the seedling a parsley- or celery-like appearance — Golden Alexander is in the carrot family (Apiaceae), and the family resemblance to edible herbs is real. Leaves are alternate. The divided compound leaf structure sets it apart from most weeds in a treewell context.
