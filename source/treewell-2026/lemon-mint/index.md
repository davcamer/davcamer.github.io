## About

Lemon Mint — also called Lemon Bee Balm or Purple Horse Mint — is a native annual of south-central North America, particularly Texas, Oklahoma, and the surrounding region, where it grows in dry, open habitats. It belongs to the same genus as the more familiar garden bee balm (*Monarda didyma*), but has a distinctly different look: the flowers are arranged in whorled tiers around the stem, like stacked rings of lavender-pink blossoms, giving the plant an unusually architectural appearance. The foliage has a strong lemon scent when crushed. Plants grow 1–3 feet tall.

## Wildlife Value

Lemon Mint is a magnet for native bees of many species, butterflies, and hummingbirds. The lemon-scented foliage may help deter some pest insects while attracting beneficial ones. As a member of the bee balm family, it shares the genus's strong reputation as one of the top pollinator-supporting plant groups in North American gardens.

## In the Treewell

Lemon Mint is an annual that germinates and blooms quickly from seed. The tiered, whorled flower structure is architecturally unusual and visually distinctive — the kind of plant that makes passersby stop and look more closely. It tolerates heat and drought well, suiting the summer conditions in a NYC treewell. It may self-seed in favorable spots.

## Growing Notes

- **Type:** Annual
- **Height:** 1–3 ft
- **Bloom time:** June–August
- **Sun needs:** Full sun
- **Water needs:** Low; tolerates heat and drought

## Seedling Identification

Cotyledons are small and oval. The first true leaves are oval to lance-shaped, opposite, and slightly hairy — a typical mint-family seedling appearance. The key ID feature is scent: crushing a leaf releases a distinctive bright lemon fragrance. The square stem characteristic of the mint family develops within the first few leaf pairs. If the seedling smells lemony and has a square stem, it's Lemon Mint.
