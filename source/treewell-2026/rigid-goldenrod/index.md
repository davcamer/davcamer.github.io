## About

Rigid Goldenrod is one of the most distinctive goldenrods native to central and eastern North America, standing apart from most of its relatives by producing flat-topped clusters of golden-yellow flowers rather than the arching plumes typical of the genus. It grows 1–5 feet tall with stiff, upright stems and rough, oval leaves that give the plant a solid, architectural quality. It is native to dry prairies and open areas, tolerating lean soils and full sun with ease. The flat-topped flower clusters attract pollinators from a distance and make the plant easy to identify in late summer.

## Wildlife Value

Rigid Goldenrod is an outstanding late-season pollinator plant and one of the top goldenrods for specialist insect support, hosting over 100 insect species including specialist bees and moths. The flat-topped clusters are a landing pad for a remarkable diversity of insects in late summer and fall. Seeds are eaten by sparrows, juncos, and other birds. It is a critical nectar source for monarch butterflies during fall migration.

## In the Treewell

Rigid Goldenrod's tolerance of dry, poor soils makes it a strong candidate for the lean conditions of an urban treewell. The architectural flat-topped flower clusters are a distinctive visual element in September and October. It spreads slowly by rhizome, gradually filling in space without becoming aggressive. A top-tier late-season wildlife plant that earns its keep.

## Growing Notes

- **Type:** Perennial
- **Height:** 1–5 ft
- **Bloom time:** August–October
- **Sun needs:** Full sun
- **Water needs:** Low; tolerates dry, lean soils

## Seedling Identification

Cotyledons are small and oval. The first true leaves are broadly oval — noticeably wider than Gray Goldenrod's leaves — with a rough, almost sandpapery texture and an alternate arrangement. The combination of broad oval shape and rough hairy texture is the key. Rigid Goldenrod leaves are among the widest of the goldenrods, which sets it apart from other similar-looking seedlings. The leaves are a medium gray-green from the hairs covering them.
