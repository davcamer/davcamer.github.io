## About

Blue Flax is a graceful, airy perennial native to the western regions of North America, from Alaska down through California and into the Great Plains. It's a close relative of European cultivated flax (the source of linen and linseed oil), sharing its delicate five-petaled flowers, but in a lovely sky-blue rather than the cultivated plant's pale lavender. Plants reach 12–24 inches tall with wispy, narrow foliage. The individual flowers open each morning and close — or drop their petals — by afternoon, but the plant produces new blooms continuously over many weeks.

## Wildlife Value

Blue Flax attracts native bees and small butterflies with its pollen and nectar. Sparrows and finches eat the seeds when they ripen in late summer. Because the flowers close in the afternoon, they're most alive in the morning hours when pollinator activity tends to be highest.

## In the Treewell

Blue Flax is a self-seeding plant that establishes readily in poor, dry soils — conditions that match a typical NYC treewell well. The delicate flowers and fine-textured foliage add an airy quality that contrasts nicely with the bolder wildflowers around it. Once established, it should return each year from self-sown seed, weaving itself through the planting.

## Growing Notes

- **Type:** Perennial
- **Height:** 12–24 in
- **Bloom time:** April–July
- **Sun needs:** Full sun
- **Water needs:** Low; prefers dry to medium soils

## Seedling Identification

Cotyledons are narrow and slightly strap-shaped. The first true leaves are very narrow and linear — almost needle-like — with a smooth texture and a blue-green color. The alternate leaf arrangement and slender form can make this look like a grass seedling at a quick glance, but the leaves are clearly flat, not folded at the midrib, and they lack the ligule (small membrane at the leaf base) that grasses have. Look for a slender, upright seedling with wiry stems and a notably blue-green cast.
