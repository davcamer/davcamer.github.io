*Lupinus perennis*

**Seed Mixes:** Eastern Pollinator

## About

Perennial Lupine — also called Wild Lupine or Sundial Lupine — is the only lupine species native to the eastern United States, found in sandy, well-drained soils in open pine woods and clearings from Maine to Florida. It grows 1–2 feet tall and produces tall, handsome spikes of blue (occasionally white or pink) pea-like flowers in May and June. The attractive palmate leaves, with leaflets radiating like fingers from a central point, are ornamental throughout the season. Like all legumes, it fixes nitrogen in the soil. It does not transplant well and is best established from directly sown seed.

## Wildlife Value

Perennial Lupine is the sole larval host plant of the Karner Blue butterfly, a federally endangered species whose decline is directly linked to the loss of wild lupine habitat. It also supports specialist native bees including lupine bees (*Lupinus*-specialist *Andrena*) and other ground-nesting bees. The nitrogen-fixing root nodules improve soil quality for the surrounding plant community.

## In the Treewell

Perennial Lupine requires well-drained, preferably sandy or lean soil — it will struggle in heavy clay. Urban treewells, with their typically lean and well-drained conditions, may actually suit it well. It can be slow to establish and may not bloom in its first year, but it's worth the patience for its ecological value and beauty. Once established, do not disturb it — the deep taproot does not appreciate being moved.

## Growing Notes

- **Type:** Perennial
- **Height:** 1–2 ft
- **Bloom time:** May–June
- **Sun needs:** Full sun
- **Water needs:** Low; requires good drainage; dislikes heavy clay

## Seedling Identification

Cotyledons are large, oval, and noticeably thick and fleshy. The first true leaves are the most distinctive of any plant in the mix: they are palmately compound, with 7–11 narrow leaflets radiating outward from a central point — like the fingers of an open hand or a small umbrella. This palm-shaped compound leaf is unmistakable at any growth stage. The leaves are smooth and a bright green. Even a tiny seedling with one true leaf looks like a miniature version of the adult plant.
