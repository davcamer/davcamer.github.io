*Agastache foeniculum*

**Seed Mixes:** Eastern Pollinator

## About

Lavender Hyssop — also called Anise Hyssop — is a perennial native to north-central North America, found in dry prairies and open areas of the upper Midwest and Great Plains. It grows 2–4 feet tall and produces dense, upright spikes of lavender-blue flowers from midsummer into early fall. Both the flowers and leaves carry a pleasant anise-like fragrance — the foliage can be used in teas and as a culinary herb. Though individual plants are short-lived, the plant self-seeds so freely that it effectively persists indefinitely in any spot where it's happy.

## Wildlife Value

Lavender Hyssop is consistently rated among the best all-around pollinator plants for North American gardens. Bumblebees work the flowers intensively, hummingbirds visit regularly, and dozens of butterfly species are attracted to the long-blooming spikes. Honeybees find it irresistible. The aromatic foliage may also deter some pest insects. Seeds are eaten by birds after the bloom period ends.

## In the Treewell

Once established, Lavender Hyssop will act as a reliable perennial in the treewell through self-seeding even if individual plants are short-lived. The fragrant foliage is a nice feature near a building entrance. The lavender-blue spikes contrast beautifully with the yellows and oranges of the summer wildflowers in the mix. It tends to thrive in lean soils and does not need fertilizing.

## Growing Notes

- **Type:** Perennial (short-lived, self-seeding)
- **Height:** 2–4 ft
- **Bloom time:** July–September
- **Sun needs:** Full sun
- **Water needs:** Low to moderate; prefers well-drained soils

## Seedling Identification

Cotyledons are small and oval. The first true leaves are broadly oval to slightly heart-shaped, opposite, and smooth. The definitive identification feature is scent: crush a leaf gently between your fingers and it releases a strong, sweet anise or licorice fragrance. No common weed smells like this. The square stem of the mint family develops early. If it smells like licorice, it's Lavender Hyssop.
