*Coreopsis tinctoria*

**Seed Mixes:** Bird Lover's, Eastern Pollinator, Northeast Native

---

## About

Plains Coreopsis is a cheerful, fast-growing annual native to central North America that has naturalized widely across the continent and beyond. It produces an abundance of bright yellow daisy-like flowers with distinctive dark red-brown centers and often red-banded inner petals on plants ranging from 1–4 feet tall. It is one of the most reliable and easy wildflower annuals available, blooming heavily and quickly from seed. The name *tinctoria* (meaning "used in dyeing") refers to the plant's historical use as a source of yellow-orange dye.

## Wildlife Value

Plains Coreopsis produces abundant nectar that supports bees, butterflies, and beetles throughout the summer. The seeds are eaten by many songbirds. Specialist Coreopsis-mining bees (*Calliopsis* species) depend on coreopsis pollen for their larval food supply, making this plant particularly valuable for supporting these specialist bees in the urban landscape.

## In the Treewell

Plains Coreopsis is one of those plants that earns its place by being reliably excellent without demanding anything in return. It blooms quickly and heavily from seed, tolerates a wide range of soils, and self-seeds prolifically to return in subsequent years. It keeps blooming through summer heat that would stop other plants, making it a reliable "filler" that maintains color and nectar supply when other plants are struggling.

## Growing Notes

- **Type:** Annual
- **Height:** 1–4 ft
- **Bloom time:** June–September
- **Sun needs:** Full sun
- **Water needs:** Low; tolerates heat, drought, and poor soils

## Seedling Identification

Cotyledons are small, oval to slightly oblong. The first true leaves are opposite and deeply divided — pinnately cut into narrow segments that give the leaf a feathery or comb-like appearance. This finely divided opposite leaf structure at an early seedling stage is quite distinctive. Lance-Leaved Coreopsis (also in the mix) has simple, undivided leaves at the same stage, which is the easiest way to tell the two apart. Smooth texture, bright green color.
