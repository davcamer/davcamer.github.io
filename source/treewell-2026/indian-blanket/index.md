## About

Indian Blanket — also called Firewheel — is a bold, heat-loving annual native to central and southern North America and parts of South America. It grows 1–2 feet tall and produces an abundance of daisy-like flowers with distinctive fringed petals in fiery combinations of red and yellow, sometimes yellow throughout. The plant is extraordinarily tolerant of heat and drought, having evolved in the open, sun-baked prairies and sandy soils of the Great Plains and Southwest. It blooms from early summer until frost without much fuss.

## Wildlife Value

Indian Blanket's long bloom season provides a sustained source of nectar and pollen for bees and butterflies through the hottest months of summer. Goldfinches and other small birds eat the seeds as the flowers go to seed, and the plant provides seed resources into fall. Because it appears in three of the four mixes planted here, it will be well represented in the treewell and should contribute substantially to pollinator activity all summer.

## In the Treewell

Indian Blanket is one of the most reliable performers in hot, dry urban conditions — a strong candidate to be a standout plant in the treewell. The bold red-and-yellow coloration is visually striking and highly visible from the street. It self-seeds readily, so a good colony may establish itself in the treewell over subsequent seasons.

## Growing Notes

- **Type:** Annual
- **Height:** 1–2 ft
- **Bloom time:** June–October
- **Sun needs:** Full sun
- **Water needs:** Low; excellent drought and heat tolerance

## Seedling Identification

Cotyledons are oval. The first true leaves are spatula- to lance-shaped, alternate, grayish-green, and covered in short rough hairs that give the surface a slightly sandpapery feel. The grayish cast from the fine hairs and the rough texture are the key features. Leaf margins may be slightly wavy or have a few shallow teeth. The seedlings can look a bit like young Black-Eyed Susan, but Indian Blanket leaves tend to be more spatula-shaped (wider at the tip) and grayer.
