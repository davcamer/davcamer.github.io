*Liatris spicata*

**Seed Mixes:** Bird Lover's, Northeast Native

## About

Gayfeather — also called Blazing Star or Dense Blazing Star — is a striking perennial native to moist meadows and prairies of eastern North America. It grows 2–4 feet tall from a corm (a small bulb-like structure underground), producing dense, upright spikes of rosy-purple flower heads. One of the most distinctive things about Gayfeather is that its flowers open from the top of the spike downward — the opposite of most spike-flowered plants, which open from the bottom up. It blooms in late summer through fall, providing color and nectar precisely when many other plants are winding down.

## Wildlife Value

Gayfeather is a monarch butterfly magnet during the fall migration south, and it's hard to overstate how valuable that makes it in late summer and early fall. Native bees, swallowtail butterflies, skippers, and bumblebees also flock to the flowers. Goldfinches eat the seeds from the dried stalks — leave them standing through winter rather than cutting them back.

## In the Treewell

Gayfeather grows from a corm and takes a year or two to establish, but once it settles in it is reliably long-lived and drought tolerant. The upright spikes are a bold vertical accent in the treewell, visible from a distance. As a fall bloomer that overlaps with monarch migration timing, it's one of the most ecologically impactful plants in the entire mix.

## Growing Notes

- **Type:** Perennial
- **Height:** 2–4 ft
- **Bloom time:** July–September
- **Sun needs:** Full sun
- **Water needs:** Low to moderate; drought tolerant once established

## Seedling Identification

Cotyledons are narrow. The first true leaves are very narrow and grass-like — linear, alternate, and smooth with a slightly firm texture. At first glance this can look like a grass seedling, but look closely: the leaves are alternate (not in two ranks like grasses), slightly thicker and more rigid than grass blades, and lack the ligule at the base. The seedling grows from a small corm just below the soil surface. Growth is slow in year one as the plant focuses on corm development.
