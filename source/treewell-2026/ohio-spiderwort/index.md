*Tradescantia ohiensis*

**Seed Mixes:** Eastern Pollinator, Northeast Native

## About

Ohio Spiderwort is a native perennial of eastern and central North America, found in open woods, prairies, roadsides, and meadow edges. It forms a clumping plant 2–3 feet tall with long, lance-shaped, blue-green leaves and clusters of three-petaled flowers in shades of blue-violet to pink. Each individual flower lasts only a single morning — opening at dawn and withering by afternoon — but the plant produces new blooms continuously for weeks. After the main bloom period, the plant often reblooms in fall if cut back. The foliage is attractive even when the plant is not flowering.

## Wildlife Value

Bumblebees are the primary pollinators of Ohio Spiderwort, using a technique called "buzz pollination" — they grab the stamens and vibrate their flight muscles at a specific frequency to shake loose the pollen. This same technique is used to pollinate tomatoes and blueberries. Other native bees also visit. Some birds eat the seeds. The clumping foliage can provide shelter for ground-level insects.

## In the Treewell

Ohio Spiderwort blooms early — May through July — filling the treewell with blue-violet color before the summer wildflowers take over. It tends to go somewhat dormant in midsummer heat; cutting the stems back by half after the first flush of bloom often encourages a second round of flowering in early fall. The blue-green foliage is ornamentally attractive throughout the season.

## Growing Notes

- **Type:** Perennial
- **Height:** 2–3 ft
- **Bloom time:** May–July; sometimes again in fall
- **Sun needs:** Full sun to part shade
- **Water needs:** Low to moderate; adaptable

## Seedling Identification

Ohio Spiderwort is one of the easier seedlings to identify. The cotyledons are narrow and strap-like. The first true leaves are long, strap-shaped, smooth, and blue-green, with parallel veins running the length of the leaf — a monocot characteristic. The leaves fold slightly at the midrib and are notably wider than grass blades. They emerge folded and unfurl as they grow. The combination of strap-shaped blue-green parallel-veined leaves that are clearly wider and more substantial than grass is distinctive.
