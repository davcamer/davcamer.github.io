*Eschscholzia californica*

**Seed Mixes:** Bird Lover's

## About

California Poppy is the official state flower of California, native to the Pacific Coast states and the western US more broadly. It's a low-growing plant of 6–12 inches with finely divided, blue-green foliage and silky, cup-shaped flowers in shades of orange, yellow, cream, and red. The flowers open in sunshine and close at night or on cloudy days. In its native range it's a perennial, but it behaves as an annual in the Northeast, completing its life cycle in a single season and seeding freely to return the following year.

## Wildlife Value

California Poppy produces pollen but no nectar — the flowers are what botanists call "pollen flowers," attracting bees that collect protein-rich pollen as a food source rather than sugary nectar. Plasterer bees and other native bees are frequent visitors. Sparrows and finches eat the small seeds that form after the distinctive elongated seed capsules split open.

## In the Treewell

California Poppy is very easy from seed, best sown directly in fall or very early spring, as it prefers cool temperatures for germination. It blooms quickly and will put on a good show in spring and early summer, though it may struggle through the hot, humid New York midsummer. Expect it to self-seed into the cracks and gravel of the treewell, returning in subsequent years from seed. It thrives in dry, poor soils — the treewell is a natural fit.

## Growing Notes

- **Type:** Annual (perennial in warm climates)
- **Height:** 6–12 in
- **Bloom time:** April–September; peaks spring and early summer
- **Sun needs:** Full sun
- **Water needs:** Low; prefers dry, lean soils

## Seedling Identification

California Poppy is one of the most distinctive wildflower seedlings available. The cotyledons are narrow and strap-like. The first true leaves are finely and repeatedly divided — almost fernlike — with a distinctive blue-green to gray-green color. Even at 2–3 leaf pairs the deeply cut, delicate, blue-green foliage is unlike any common weed in the area. The stems are also smooth and somewhat waxy-looking.
