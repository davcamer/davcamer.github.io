**Seed Mixes:** Northeast Native

## About

Spotted Joe Pye Weed is a dramatic, large native perennial of moist meadows, stream banks, and open woods in central and eastern North America. It is one of the tallest plants in this mix, reaching 4–7 feet high, with sturdy, distinctive purple-spotted stems (the *maculatum* of the scientific name) and large flat-topped domes of rosy-mauve flowers in late summer. It is a commanding presence in any planting, visible from a considerable distance. The common name may refer to a Native American herbalist named Joe Pye who used the plant medicinally, though the exact origin is debated.

## Wildlife Value

Spotted Joe Pye Weed is one of the outstanding butterfly plants of the late-summer garden. Monarch butterflies, tiger swallowtails, and fritillaries are particularly drawn to the large flower domes. Native bees, wasps, and beetles also flock to it. Goldfinches eat the seeds from the dried seedheads in fall. It serves as a host plant for several moth species, adding to its ecological depth.

## In the Treewell

As the tallest plant in the mix, Spotted Joe Pye Weed may need staking in a windy urban location, or it could be positioned against the building wall for support. It prefers some moisture, so it may benefit from supplemental watering during dry spells in the treewell. Despite the care it may need, its exceptional wildlife value and dramatic appearance make it a worthwhile focal point in the planting.

## Growing Notes

- **Type:** Perennial
- **Height:** 4–7 ft
- **Bloom time:** July–September
- **Sun needs:** Full sun to part shade
- **Water needs:** Moderate to high; prefers consistently moist soil

## Seedling Identification

Cotyledons are oval. The first true leaves are lance-shaped with toothed margins, and may appear opposite or already beginning to form small whorls (groups of 3–4 leaves at the same point on the stem) — the whorled arrangement is a hallmark of Joe Pye Weed and develops early. The most distinctive feature is scent: crushing a leaf releases a sweet vanilla-like fragrance that is quite unmistakable. The combination of whorled or opposite toothed leaves with a vanilla scent is definitive.
