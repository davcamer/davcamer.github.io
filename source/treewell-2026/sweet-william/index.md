## About

Sweet William is a classic cottage garden flower from southern Europe and Asia, long cultivated in gardens around the world. It is a biennial or short-lived perennial growing 18–24 inches tall, producing dense, domed clusters of small flowers in vivid and intricate patterns — deep red, pink, white, and bicolor combinations with fringed or serrated petal edges. The flowers are deliciously spicy-sweet in fragrance. It has been cultivated in European gardens since at least the 16th century and was long a fixture of English cottage gardens before spreading to American ones. As a biennial, it forms a leafy rosette in its first year and blooms in its second.

## Wildlife Value

Sweet William's densely packed, nectar-rich flower clusters attract hummingbirds, butterflies, and moths. The tubular flower structure is particularly well suited to long-tongued pollinators. Hummingbird moths (sphinx moths) are frequent visitors in the evening. The plant will self-seed in favorable conditions to maintain itself across seasons.

## In the Treewell

Sweet William adds an old-fashioned cottage garden quality to the wildflower mix — a pleasant contrast with the more informal, naturalistic feel of the native wildflowers. It blooms in late spring, filling the treewell with fragrance and color before the summer heat arrives. As a biennial, its presence in the planting will build over the first two years as the first generation of seedlings grows to blooming size.

## Growing Notes

- **Type:** Biennial (or short-lived perennial)
- **Height:** 18–24 in
- **Bloom time:** May–July
- **Sun needs:** Full sun to part shade
- **Water needs:** Moderate; prefers well-drained, average soils

## Seedling Identification

Cotyledons are narrow and strap-like — quite elongated. The first true leaves are narrow and lance-shaped, opposite, smooth, and a distinctive blue-green color with a somewhat waxy appearance. The parallel venation on the narrow leaves and the blue-green waxy color give Sweet William seedlings a slightly grassy look, but the leaves are wider, more substantial, and clearly paired (opposite). The overall impression is a neat, upright seedling with blue-green paired strap leaves.
