## About

Showy Tick Trefoil is a native perennial legume found in moist thickets, forest edges, and prairies of eastern and central North America. It grows 3–6 feet tall — one of the taller plants in this mix — and produces loose, graceful racemes of small pink-purple pea-like flowers in summer. After the flowers fade, the plant produces distinctive segmented seedpods that break apart into individual sections, each covered in tiny hooks that cling to clothing and animal fur — earning it the nickname "beggar's lice" or "stick-tights." As a legume, it fixes atmospheric nitrogen in the soil.

## Wildlife Value

Showy Tick Trefoil is a host plant for several skipper butterflies, including Horace's Duskywing, the Eastern Tailed-Blue, and the Zarucco Duskywing. It is an excellent nectar source for bees and butterflies during its bloom period. The sticky seedpods are the plant's dispersal mechanism, hitchhiking on passing animals (and people) to reach new locations. Some birds eat the seeds.

## In the Treewell

Showy Tick Trefoil is an underused native that deserves more garden space — its ecological value is substantial, and the flower clusters are genuinely attractive. It adds vertical structure at the back of the treewell planting, and the nitrogen-fixing roots improve soil quality for surrounding plants. Fair warning: the stick-to-your-socks seedpods are a charming (if occasionally annoying) feature of late-summer visits to the treewell.

## Growing Notes

- **Type:** Perennial
- **Height:** 3–6 ft
- **Bloom time:** July–August
- **Sun needs:** Full sun to part shade
- **Water needs:** Moderate; prefers average to moist soils

## Seedling Identification

Cotyledons are oval and slightly fleshy. The first true leaves are trifoliate — composed of three oval leaflets — alternate, and covered in short hairs that give a soft, somewhat fuzzy feel. The three-parted compound leaf looks similar to clover at first glance, but the leaflets are larger, more elongated, and more obviously hairy than clover leaflets. As the plant grows the leaflets also tend to be more pointed than clover's rounded leaflets. An alternate trifoliate leaf with hairy texture on a plant that grows vigorously is a good sign you have Showy Tick Trefoil.
