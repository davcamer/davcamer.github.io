*Cosmos sulphureus*

**Seed Mixes:** Bird Lover's

## About

Sulphur Cosmos is a Mexican and Central American native that is distinct from the more familiar white and pink *Cosmos bipinnatus* — it produces semi-double daisy-like flowers in warm shades of orange, yellow, and red rather than the pastels of its relative. Plants grow 1–3 feet tall with finely divided, somewhat coarser foliage than the common cosmos. It has naturalized in many parts of North America and is extremely easy to grow, tolerating heat, humidity, and poor soils that would stress many other flowers. It blooms from early summer until hard frost — one of the longest-blooming annuals available.

## Wildlife Value

Sulphur Cosmos is a rich nectar source for bees, butterflies, and beetles throughout its extremely long season. Goldfinches and other finches eat the seeds avidly — allowing some flowers to go to seed rather than deadheading everything will keep birds visiting through fall. It also supports soldier beetles (Cantharidae), which are beneficial predators in the garden.

## In the Treewell

Sulphur Cosmos is one of the most dependable and heat-tolerant annuals for a hot, urban situation. The bold orange and yellow flowers are vibrant and eye-catching from the street, and the plant keeps blooming right through summer heat and humidity without complaint. Self-seeds readily to return in subsequent years. An excellent choice for consistent, long-season color.

## Growing Notes

- **Type:** Annual
- **Height:** 1–3 ft
- **Bloom time:** June–frost
- **Sun needs:** Full sun
- **Water needs:** Low to moderate; heat and drought tolerant

## Seedling Identification

Cotyledons are narrow and slightly elongated. The first true leaves are pinnately divided — cut into segments — but the segments are noticeably broader and more lance-shaped than the ultra-fine thread-like segments of white/pink Cosmos (Cosmos bipinnatus). The leaves are alternate, smooth, and bright green. At 2–3 leaf pairs the divided leaves are already quite recognizable as a Cosmos relative, and the broader segments (compared to the wispy white Cosmos sold as cut flowers) help distinguish this species.
