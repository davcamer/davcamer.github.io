## About

Purple Coneflower is one of the most widely planted and beloved native perennials in North American horticulture, and its popularity is entirely deserved. Native to central and eastern North America in open woods, prairies, and meadows, it grows 2–5 feet tall and produces large, showy flowers with purple-pink ray petals drooping gracefully around a spiny orange-brown central cone. The flowers bloom from June through October, and the distinctive seedheads persist through winter, providing food for birds. It is highly adaptable, long-lived, and virtually maintenance-free once established.

## Wildlife Value

Purple Coneflower is a major nectar source for bees, butterflies, and hummingbirds throughout its long bloom season. After the flowers fade, goldfinches are strongly attracted to the spiny seedheads — leaving them standing through winter is one of the most impactful things you can do for backyard birds. It is also a host plant for the Silvery Checkerspot butterfly. With all three mixes including it, Purple Coneflower will be one of the dominant plants in the treewell.

## In the Treewell

Purple Coneflower is adaptable enough to handle urban heat and drought once established, though it benefits from some supplemental watering in its first year while its root system develops. Leave the seedheads standing through winter for the birds, and cut them back in early spring. It is a truly long-lived perennial that will reward the treewell for years to come.

## Growing Notes

- **Type:** Perennial
- **Height:** 2–5 ft
- **Bloom time:** June–October
- **Sun needs:** Full sun to part shade
- **Water needs:** Low to moderate; drought tolerant once established

## Seedling Identification

Cotyledons are oval and fairly large. The first true leaves are broadly oval to lance-shaped, alternate, and notably rough — covered in stiff hairs that make the surface feel sandpapery. Three prominent veins run from the base toward the tip of the leaf (three-nerved), which is a useful detail to confirm. The rough, sandpapery, three-veined leaf is Purple Coneflower's signature feature at the seedling stage. It's similar to Black-Eyed Susan, but the leaves tend to be broader and even rougher.
